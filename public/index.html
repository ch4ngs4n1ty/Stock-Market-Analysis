What do these metrics mean? — quick reference
Universe & Discovery
Phase 1 universe: S&P 500 only Quality first
Every candidate must be a current S&P 500 constituent. The index committee already screens for liquidity, market cap, and financial viability — so this filter eliminates penny stocks, OTC names, leveraged ETPs, and speculative micro-caps in one step.
Why it matters: cleaner technical signals (real volume → real trend), more trustworthy fundamentals, and lower manipulation/pump-and-dump risk. The goal is "high-quality companies temporarily trading at attractive prices," not random explosive small caps.
How candidates are picked
Each scan pulls today's biggest market losers and intersects them with the S&P 500. On calm days when few S&P 500 names are dipping, the pool is padded with a deterministic daily rotation through the index so the board is never empty.
Future expansion: Nasdaq-100, sector ETFs, mid-caps, international equities — the universe layer is modular, so adding a new pool is one new function.
Dip Timing — "Is this a good time to buy?"
Dip from High Reward if > 5%
How far the current price sits below its recent peak (the higher of the 20-day or 50-day high).
Why it matters: a meaningful dip is what creates the entry point. A 1% dip is just noise; a 10–20% dip in a strong company is what we're hunting for.
RSI (14) Reward if < 35
Relative Strength Index — momentum on a 0–100 scale over the last 14 days. Below 30 is "oversold", above 70 is "overbought".
Why it matters: a low RSI confirms the dip is statistically deep, not just a small wobble. Stocks frequently bounce from oversold territory.
Trend (SMA 20 / 50) Reward if price > SMA 50
Compares the current price to the 20-day and 50-day Simple Moving Averages. Above both = uptrend; below both = downtrend; mixed = transitional.
Why it matters: buying a dip inside an uptrend has historically had much better odds than buying into a falling knife. This filter avoids stocks in long-term decline.
Bad-news Flag Reward if no flag
Keyword scan of recent headlines for terms like "lawsuit", "downgrade", "fraud", "investigation", "cuts guidance", "recall", "data breach".
Why it matters: a flag doesn't mean disaster, but it tells you the dip might have a real reason behind it. Always read the actual headlines before acting.
Fundamental Quality — "Is this a good company to buy?"
P/E Ratio Pass if < 20 Price ÷ EPS
Price-to-Earnings ratio. How many dollars you pay for $1 of the company's annual earnings.
Why it matters: the most common valuation gauge. A P/E of 15 means it would take 15 years of current earnings to "earn back" the share price. Lower = cheaper, but extremely low P/Es can also signal trouble — context matters.
PEG Ratio Pass if < 1 P/E ÷ earnings growth %
Price/Earnings-to-Growth. Adjusts P/E for how fast earnings are growing.
Why it matters: a high P/E can be justified if the company is growing fast. PEG < 1 generally means you're paying a fair price for the growth — a classic "growth at a reasonable price" signal popularized by Peter Lynch.
Calculated PEGs (marked with *): when no vendor PEG is available we derive it as P/E ÷ growth %. Fallback chain: 5Y EPS CAGR → 3Y EPS CAGR → TTM EPS YoY → 5Y revenue CAGR → TTM revenue YoY. If none are usable, PEG is shown as N/A and excluded from scoring (no fabricated values).
ROE Pass if > 15% Net Income ÷ Shareholder Equity
Return on Equity. How efficiently the company turns shareholder money into profit.
Why it matters: a top quality marker. Sustained ROE above 15% usually points to a durable competitive advantage — Buffett famously screens for this. Low ROE means management isn't generating much profit per dollar of equity invested.
P/B Ratio Pass if < 3 Price ÷ Book Value per Share
Price-to-Book. How much you pay relative to the company's net asset value on the balance sheet.
Why it matters: tells you how much "asset cushion" you get per dollar invested. Below 3 is reasonable for most industries (asset-light tech firms can run higher; banks and industrials lower). Useful as a downside-protection check.
D/E Ratio Pass if < 1 Total Debt ÷ Shareholder Equity
Debt-to-Equity. Measures financial leverage — how much of the company's funding comes from debt vs. equity.
Why it matters: debt amplifies both gains and losses. A D/E above 1 means the company owes more than its equity base, which makes it more fragile during downturns and rising-rate environments.
Scores & Final Rating
Dip Score 0 – 100
Sum of four technical checks: dip depth (+30), trend above SMA 50 (+25), RSI < 35 (+20), no bad-news flag (+25).
75+ = GOOD DIP · 50–74 = WATCH · < 50 = AVOID
Fundamental Score passed ÷ available × 100
Percentage of available metrics that pass. Missing data ("N/A") is excluded from both sides of the ratio, so a stock with PEG missing and the other 4 passing scores 100% — not 80%.
100% = Instant Buy Quality · 80–99% = Strong Quality · 60–79% = Decent · 40–59% = Weak · <40% = Avoid
Combined Score (Dip / 100 × 50) + (Fund% / 100 × 50)
Equal-weighted blend of timing and quality, capped at 100. Used to rank the cards top to bottom.
How to read it: a high combined score means both the entry point and the company quality look good. A high score from one side alone is interesting but not conclusive — read the Final Rating to see which case applies.
Final Rating
Combines the dip and fundamental signals into one verdict.
Instant Buy — strong dip + 100% fundamentals pass.
Strong Buy Candidate — strong dip + 80–99% pass.
Good Dip, But Needs More Research — strong dip + 60–79% pass.
Great Company, Bad Entry — 80%+ fundamentals but weak technical setup; consider waiting.
Value Trap Risk — price has dropped, but <60% of fundamentals pass.
Hold / Mixed Signals — neither side gives a clear case (or no fundamental data was available).
Avoid — both timing and quality are weak.
This is not financial advice. This tool is only a stock screening system. Always review news, earnings, industry trends, and personal risk before investing.
